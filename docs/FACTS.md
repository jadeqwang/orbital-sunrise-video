# FACTS: Voskhod 2 fact-check (researched 2026-09-28)

Confidence tags: **[solid]** = several independent sources agree · **[contested]** = sources conflict · **[weak]** = one source or a retelling only.
Network note: WebFetch and curl to Wikipedia, Smithsonian, RussianSpaceWeb, Flickr and Wikimedia Commons were blocked by the egress proxy. The facts below come from search-result excerpts, so check the URLs before publishing a quote.

> **Warning about `media/research/`:** every image in that folder was **generated by Nano Banana** (see `media/genlog.jsonl`, tag `research`). None of them is an archival photo. `leonov_orbital_sunrise_drawing.jpg` in particular is a fabrication. It has a Cyrillic signature and date, the title "Орбитальный восход", constellations and a detailed map of Europe. None of that is known on the real sketch. Don't show it or describe it as Leonov's drawing.

## 1. Suit pressure and the airlock

- **Settings [solid]:** the Berkut had two pressure modes, **0.40 atm nominal and 0.27 atm emergency**, set by the suit's regulator and relief valve. The control was **on the suit, not on the KP-55 backpack.** The backpack held the oxygen supply.
- **Control [contested on the details]:** Rostec says Leonov "turned the pressure regulator" ("повернул регулятор давления"). One Russian description puts the regulator **at the solar plexus, as a small blue tap ("синий краник")** [weak]. English retellings say "valve", "a valve in its lining" or "lever". In Leonov's own words (MK, 2004): "breaking every instruction and telling the ground nothing, I switch to 0.27 atm". He says he switched to the suit's **second mode**, which reads as one deliberate switch, not repeated bleeding. Some retellings say he let the pressure down gradually [weak].
- **Head-first [contested]:** in his memoir (*Two Sides of the Moon*, 2004) Leonov says he went in **head-first**, against the plan. Then, still in the suit, he had to curl round inside the ~1 m-wide, ~2.5 m-long Volga tube to face and close the outer hatch. He called this "tremendous effort". **His immediate 1965 post-flight report says he went in legs-first**, with the camera in his right hand (Air & Space/Smithsonian, 2020). The head-first story is the famous one, but nitpickers may know the report.
- **Struggle [solid]:** as the suit swelled, **his fingertips no longer reached the glove tips and his feet floated inside the boots.** He was soaked in sweat ("up to his knees", often put at ~6 L), his core temperature rose by **~1.8 °C**, and his heart rate is cited at up to **~190 bpm** [weak on the exact figure]. EVA time was 12 min 9 s.
- **On screen:** white Berkut, gloved hand **twisting a small tap on the chest** (not the backpack), the suit visibly slackening, gloves going limp at the fingertips. Head-first into a pale fabric tube, then a knotted, cramped turn with elbows and knees jammed against the walls, and a sweat-fogged visor.
- Sources: [Air & Space 2020](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/turns-out-alexei-leonovs-first-spacewalk-wasnt-quite-dramatic-we-thought-180974522/) · [Astronautix: Berkut](http://www.astronautix.com/b/berkut.html) · [Rostec](https://rostec.ru/media/news/aleksey-leonov-i-ego-berkut/) · [Presidential Library (quotes MK 2004)](https://www.prlib.ru/news/1292617) · [Nightmare of Voskhod 2](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/the-nightmare-of-voskhod-2-8655378/) · [Space.com](https://space.com/amp/28858-first-spacewalk-alexei-leonov-50th-anniversary.html)

## 2. After landing: what they wore

- **No orange uniforms. That idea is wrong.** They landed in their **white Berkut suits** [solid]. The orange coverall is Gagarin's Vostok SK-1, from a different programme.
- **In the capsule and by the fire [solid]:** sweat sloshed in Leonov's suit (knee-deep, about 6 L). They **stripped naked, wrung out their underwear and put it back on.** They pulled the rigid outer layers off the suits, wore the soft **thermal lining/insulation**, and wrapped themselves in it, **tied round with parachute cord**. They could not pull the snagged parachute down for extra cover.
- **Air drops [solid]:** helicopters dropped **fur boots (wolf-fur *unty*), sheepskin half-coats, trousers and jackets, an axe, food and thermoses**, plus a cognac bottle that is said to have broken. Most of it snagged in trees or sank in the snow. They recovered **the boots**.
- **Rescue [solid]:** skiers reached them on the **morning of 20 March**. The rescuers **dressed them in warm, dry clothes**, fed them, lit a big fire and built a log hut. One source says they also gave them a hot-water "bath" in a cauldron dropped from a helicopter [weak]. On **21 March** they **skied** to a clearing (~5–9 km; sources vary) and flew by helicopter to Perm.
- **On screen:** (a) **Climbing out:** white suits and white helmets, waist-deep snow, dense spruce/birch taiga, a red/orange-and-white parachute draped in the treetops, grey late-afternoon light, −25 °C. (b) **First night:** helmets off, men in damp underwear bundled in pale quilted/silvery suit liners bound with cord, fur boots, a small fire beside the scorched sphere with its hatch gone. (c) **Rescue:** strangers on skis in dark sheepskin coats; the cosmonauts in borrowed coats, fur hats and felt boots (*valenki*), then all of them skiing out in single file. Check the rescue-day garments against the Perm Archive photos before locking the design [weak].
- Sources: [Rodina 2026 (declassified Perm archive)](https://rodina-history.ru/2026/07/24/kak-kosmonavta-leonova-spasali-v-tajge-sekretnyj-arhiv-pokazali-ego-docheri.html) · [KP.ru](https://www.kp.ru/daily/27289.3/4425913/) · [Science Museum blog](https://blog.sciencemuseum.org.uk/remembering-alexei-leonov/) · [Astronautix Voskhod 2](http://www.astronautix.com/v/voskhod2.html) · [Uralsky Sledopyt 2025](https://uralstalker.com/uarch/us/2025/04/16/)

## 3. The hatch and the tree

- **[solid]** The hatch is blown by **explosive bolts**. It jammed against a large tree; retellings of Leonov's memoir call it **a birch** [weak on the species]. The crew **rocked the hatch back and forth**, and **Belyayev shoved it free.** It **fell away into the snow**, so the capsule stayed open all night.
- Landing: **19 Mar 1965, 09:02 UTC**, taiga of the Upper Kama upland, Usolsky district, **Perm Oblast (the Urals, not Siberia).** It was about 386 km from the target by one count; other sources say "~2,000 km downrange" [contested]. There were two nights in the forest.
- **On screen:** hatch cover wedged against a pale birch trunk, two pairs of gloved hands heaving it rhythmically, then it drops into deep snow.

## 4. Leonov's real "Orbital Sunrise" drawing

- **Medium [solid]:** **coloured pencils** (Soviet *Taktika* brand, tied on threads, held to his wrist with a rubber band) on a **loose sheet of white paper**. The image is small, **about the length of one pencil**, and **does not fill the sheet**. Traditionally dated **18 March 1965** and said to be drawn aboard Voskhod 2. It is held at the Gagarin Cosmonaut Training Centre museum and was shown at the Science Museum, London, in 2015. Nothing independent documents that it was drawn in orbit rather than soon after [weak]. A separate **post-landing** Leonov drawing also exists (MK, 2016).
- **Composition [solid in outline, weak in detail]:** a **curved horizon arc** with **Earth below it in dark blue and black**, indicated only by the curve, with no continents. Leonov described the bands above the limb, bottom to top: **bright red → orange → light blue → blue → violet → velvety black.** The **Sun is a red ball emerging from a field of blue.** The top is a deep swathe of black. It is fairly abstract. **No reliable source mentions stars, continents, a spacecraft, or a title, signature or date written on the sheet.** Treat any of those as invented.
- **Image:** I found no Wikimedia Commons file; it is probably still under copyright. The best-known reproduction is the Gagarin CTC museum photo, reposted on [Flickr](https://www.flickr.com/photos/199284959@N05/54325336951). **The download was blocked** (Flickr and Commons refused by the proxy), so there is no `leonov_drawing_real.jpg`.
- **On screen:** a small, loose pencil study with white margins, arcs across the sheet, a soft red-orange rim fading through blues into heavy black, a red sun on the rim, and no text.
- Sources: [ClimateCultures](https://climatecultures.net/museum-of-the-anthropocene/material-culture-inside-the-museum/orbital-sunrise-sketch-alexei-leonov/) · [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/the-first-artwork-made-in-outer-space/) · [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/first-drawing-in-space-cosmonauts-science-museum) · [TechInsider (Leonov's colour description)](https://www.techinsider.ru/technologies/1564565-pervyy-risunok-v-kosmose-sdelal-sovetskiy-kosmonavt-posmotrite-na-etu-kartinu/) · [Lenta 2015](https://lenta.ru/news/2015/09/01/leonov/) · [MK 2016](https://www.mk.ru/science/2016/04/12/obnarodovan-neizvestnyy-risunok-kosmonavta-leonova-sdelannyy-posle-prizemleniya.html)

## 5. Polaris Dawn EVA suit (12 Sep 2024)

- **Slimmer than the EMU? Yes [solid].** The main reason is that it has **no backpack (PLSS)**: oxygen, power and cooling come from Dragon **through an umbilical**, as on Gemini and Voskhod. The EMU weighs ~127 kg with its PLSS. SpaceX has not published an official EVA-suit mass. The trade-off is a tethered suit for short EVAs only.
- **Look:** a mostly **white** suit with dark grey/black accents and an added thermal outer layer. The helmet is a **3D-printed polycarbonate** shell with a **copper and indium-tin-oxide-coated visor** that reads bronze/gold when lowered, and it has an **in-helmet HUD** (pressure, temperature, humidity). The **boots** use the heat-resistant material of the Falcon 9 interstage. **"Skywalker"** is a truss-like frame of handholds and a foot restraint fixed in Dragon's nose-hatch opening. The whole cabin was depressurised, so all four crew wore suits. Isaacman and then Gillis each spent **~7–8 min** half out of the hatch (not free-floating) at ~700 km altitude.
- Sources: [Polaris Program](https://polarisprogram.com/polaris-dawn-crew-tests-new-suit-and-completes-first-commercial-spacewalk/) · [Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/science/spacex-eva-suits/) · [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91190436/why-spacex-suits-look-so-sleek-and-nasas-look-so-clunky) · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/09/12/spacex-performs-historic-first-spacewalk-with-polaris-dawn-crew.html)

## 7. Re-entry

- **[solid]** The automatic orientation and retrofire failed on the planned revolution. Belyayev oriented the ship by hand. He had to **lie across both seats to use the Vzor** optical sight while Leonov held him down, and getting back into his seat cost ~46 s, so the retrofire came late. The manual retrofire was **one revolution later**; Wikipedia counts it as the 18th, and sources number the orbits differently.
- **[solid]** The **instrument module did not separate cleanly.** The joined stack **tumbled** until the connecting cable burned through at ~100 km.
- **G-load [weak]:** retellings often cite ~10 g, typical of a ballistic Vostok/Voskhod re-entry. I found no primary figure.
- **On screen:** through the porthole the view strobes: blue-white Earth, black sky, a hard white sun-flash, over and over. Then an orange plasma glow builds on the glass as the spin slows after the cable breaks. The crew are pressed into their couches with faces sagging.
- Sources: [Astronautix](http://www.astronautix.com/v/voskhod2.html) · [Drew Ex Machina](https://www.drewexmachina.com/2015/03/18/the-mission-of-voskhod-2/) · [Wikipedia Voskhod 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voskhod_2)
