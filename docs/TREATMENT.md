# ORBITAL SUNRISE — music video treatment

> The first artwork ever made in space was a colored-pencil sketch of a sunrise,
> drawn by a man who almost didn't make it home. This video is drawn the same way.

## The idea in one breath

On 18 March 1965 Alexei Leonov stepped out of **Voskhod‑2** — *Восход*, literally
**"Sunrise"** — and became the first human being in open space. Everything that could go
wrong did: his suit ballooned until he could not get back in, he bled his own air out to
fit through the airlock, the automatic guidance failed, the capsule spun on re-entry,
they overshot by hundreds of kilometres and came down in the snow of the Ural taiga.
Somewhere in the middle of all that, with colored pencils tied to his wrist on a string,
he drew the orbital sunrise. The drawing survived.

So the video is **a drawing that survives**. Every frame is redrawn in colored pencil by
code — rotoscoped over filmed (generated) performance plates the viewer never sees.
Space is **black paper**: the void is the paper itself, and light is added stroke by
stroke, the way you draw with a white pencil in the dark. Earth is **white paper**: when
the capsule lands, the page turns white, and the snow is the paper.

Two hands draw the story. **Leonov's**, in 1965, with pencils on a string. And **the
singer's**, now, on Earth — *"I'll never float where the sky turns red … can't think,
can't write, but my hands still know."* The film cuts between the two until, at the
landing, they are the same drawing.

## Why this look (and not action-movie slop)

* **It is the true medium of the story.** Leonov's pencils are the reason the song
  exists (John Green's *Orbital Sunrise* and the found poem it became). The style *is*
  the theme: art as a survival technique.
* **It reads instantly on a phone.** Luminous colored strokes on black are
  high-contrast, graphic and unlike anything else on the timeline. Pencil hatching boils
  like hand-drawn animation, so even a still moment is alive.
* **Recognisable anchors.** a-ha *Take On Me* (pencil rotoscope), Spider-Verse (mixed
  line and color systems), *Gravity* (the climb home), *Apollo 11* (2019) and *For All
  Mankind* (mission-control telemetry), *Interstellar* / *Project Hail Mary* (the lone
  competent human doing math against physics), K-pop MVs (attention choreography:
  cuts on the beat, symmetrical hero frames, huge bilingual type, member ID cards).
* **The emotion comes from the strokes, not explosions.** Calm = few long strokes,
  drawn on threes. Panic = dense red scribble, drawn on ones. The drawing hand itself
  is the performance.

## Visual language

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Paper | Black paper for space and the capsule. Warm white paper for Earth, snow and the present day. The switch happens exactly once, at the landing. |
| Pencils | A fixed Soviet-box palette: white, silver-grey, cobalt, ultramarine, sky, vermilion, orange, cadmium gold, red, graphite, black. Nothing outside the box. |
| Light | Drawn, never glowed. A sunrise is a storm of radial orange/gold strokes; a rim light is a thin warm hatch along an edge. |
| Line | Confident contours with overshoot, redrawn every drawing (boil). Tone is etching-style layered hatching; glows use flow-following stroke clouds. |
| Frame rate of drawing | Threes (8 dwg/s) = awe, stillness. Twos (12) = default. Ones (24) = panic, impact. |
| Color arc | Monochrome white-on-black → first gold at the first sunrise → red scribble in the airlock → blue orbit → the page goes white in the snow → full color at home. |
| Type | Heavy condensed sans for impact lyrics (English) with red Cyrillic under-lines; serif italic for the singer's intimate lines; mono telemetry for mission data; pencil handwriting for "Art is a landing in the snow". |
| Composition | When lyrics are big, the plate is quiet on that side: characters sit right, words sit left (and vice versa). Subtitle mode during dense storytelling. |

## Arc (sections of the song)

| Time | Section | What we see |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 – 0:22 | Intro (whispered) | A poster frame draws itself in 2 s — the hook. Then darkness: the first man outside, tiny against the Earth. *First man floating in the void of space / Can't feel his hands, can't feel his face.* |
| 0:22 – 0:34 | Hook 1 | The first orbital sunrise ever seen by a human outside a ship. The first color in the film: gold. **ORBITAL SUNRISE** / ОРБИТАЛЬНЫЙ ВОСХОД. |
| 0:34 – 0:57 | Build + pre-chorus | The suit balloons; he can't reach the camera, can't fit the hatch. Pressure telemetry. He bleeds his air out — 0.40 → 0.27 atm — and goes in head-first (after a feet-first try jams, in the alternate cut's N2; the 1965 report says legs first). "Eternity inside the airlock door" (was "Ninety minutes"). Red scribble closes in. |
| 0:59 – 1:10 | Chorus 2 | Inside. Relief through the porthole: another sunrise. |
| 1:10 – 1:44 | Drop 1 (vocal chops) | Orbit montage on the beat: member ID cards for LEONOV and BELYAYEV, sixteen sunrises a day, the Globus spinning, and Leonov drawing the sunrise with pencils floating on strings. |
| 1:45 – 1:57 | Breakdown vocals | The singer on Earth, under a red sky: *I'll never float where the sky turns red.* A launch far away. |
| 1:58 – 2:09 | "Art is a landing in the snow" | The quiet core. White paper for the first time. Two hands drawing — his and hers. |
| 2:09 – 2:20 | Build | *Guidance failed and the capsule spun.* First manual re-entry: Leonov holding Belyayev across his lap at the Vzor, doing the math with a spinning sun. **HOLD ON** ×3. |
| 2:22 – 2:33 | Final chorus | Re-entry. *Burning gold* is literal: the page itself burns. |
| 2:33 – 2:56 | Drop 2 | *Off course … (but I'm) home.* Parachute, cloud, the map line running past the target. |
| 2:56 – 3:08 | Outro vocals | *Fifteen hundred klicks, coming in hot / we overshot.* Falling into endless forest. |
| 3:08 | **MADE IT DOWN** | Impact on the drop. The page turns white. Snow. |
| 3:10 – 3:45 | Instrumental | Two men, a fire, wolves' eyes, rescuers on skis — and then the legacy: the spacewalks, the handshake in orbit, the stations, the Moon again. What was impossible becomes routine. |
| 3:45 – 4:02 | End | Leonov's drawing, alone on the white page. It survived. Title, while the last chord is held and allowed to ring out (the song's final note is extended by ~4.5 s with a spectral freeze, see `tools/extend_ending.py`). |
