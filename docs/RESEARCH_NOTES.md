## The math-ring scene (2:24, "Doing the math with a spinning sun")

**Is it real? Yes.** The automatic orientation failed, so Belyayev oriented Voskhod 2 by hand
for retrofire. To see through the Vzor (the floor-mounted optical sight), he had to leave his
seat, and Leonov held him in place. In Leonov's own words:

> "Pasha began orienting the craft for reentry. This was no easy task—in order to use the
> optical device necessary for orientation, he had to lean horizontally across both seats in
> the spacecraft, while I held him steady in front of the orientation porthole."
>
> A. Leonov, "The Nightmare of Voskhod 2", *Air & Space/Smithsonian*, Jan 2005, PDF p. 4

RussianSpaceWeb (A. Zak, "Voskhod-2 lands in the wild", p. 3) agrees that Belyayev "unbuckled
from his seat and moved closer to the window" and that Leonov "also had to move". It adds that
both men were "apparently out of their seats" when the engine fired.

What is **not** supported:
- the often-repeated "46 seconds" to get back into the seat (in neither full text);
- "across his lap" (neither source says how Leonov held him);
- a spinning sun during orientation. The tumble came *after* retrofire, so the lyric is poetic.

The picture shows one cosmonaut in his seat with the other behind him: the moment before or
after the lean. The figures in the ring (Δv ≈ 155 m/s, ≈ 45 s burn, 90.9 min orbit,
167–475 km, ≈ 16 kN) are TDU-1 and orbit data from [FACTCHECK.md §4.2](FACTCHECK.md). Neither
source gives the burn length.
